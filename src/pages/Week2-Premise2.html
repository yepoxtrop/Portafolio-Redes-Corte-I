Showing problems in our hypothetical school network.

🔌01 / 07

Connectivity

Can we reach it?

THE PROBLEM

Connected to Wi-Fi. Still cannot open the classroom platform.

Look closer ＋

💬 Why it matters

Students cannot access lessons even when their device says “connected”.

🔧 Our proposal

Check the link, IP address, subnet mask, default gateway and DNS. Then test the actual learning platform.

🔎 How we would check

Test the gateway, the destination by IP, name resolution and the application. Record exactly which step fails.

Think about it: A successful ping checks reachability; it does not prove that a website or login works.

🚀02 / 07

Performance

Can it keep up?

THE PROBLEM

The class gets busy. The video call freezes.

Look closer ＋

💬 Why it matters

Teachers repeat explanations and students miss parts of the lesson.

🔧 Our proposal

Compare demand during quiet and busy periods. Check link use and Wi-Fi capacity, then size or prioritise resources where justified.

🔎 How we would check

Measure latency, packet loss and useful throughput under representative loads. Set targets based on the applications.

Think about it: More Internet bandwidth will not fix every overloaded access point or local link.

🔒03 / 07

Security

Who gets access?

THE PROBLEM

Guests and school staff share a network with weak access controls.

Look closer ＋

💬 Why it matters

School information may be exposed to people who should not access it.

🔧 Our proposal

Separate user groups with VLANs, control traffic between them and protect accounts and management access.

🔎 How we would check

Test an allowed connection and a blocked one. Confirm that guests can use approved services without reaching staff resources.

Think about it: VLAN separation alone is not a complete security policy.

🛟04 / 07

Availability

What if it breaks?

THE PROBLEM

One central device fails. Important services stop.

Look closer ＋

💬 Why it matters

Classes and office tasks can stop while the team tries to recover.

🔧 Our proposal

Identify single points of failure. Evaluate backup power, configuration backups and redundancy according to service impact and budget.

🔎 How we would check

Plan a controlled failure exercise. Record which services remain available and how long recovery takes.

Think about it: A spare device helps only if the team knows how to restore the service.

🌱05 / 07

Scalability

Room to grow?

THE PROBLEM

A new classroom opens. Ports, addresses or capacity run short.

Look closer ＋

💬 Why it matters

New users may experience delays or require disruptive changes to the network.

🔧 Our proposal

Plan address space, spare ports and capacity for expected growth. Use a phased expansion plan.

🔎 How we would check

Estimate demand for another classroom and test the proposed configuration before rollout.

Think about it: Growth planning balances future needs with today’s budget; it is not simply buying everything now.

🧰06 / 07

Management

Can we maintain it?

THE PROBLEM

A setting changed. Nobody knows what changed or why.

Look closer ＋

💬 Why it matters

The team loses time guessing, and a small incident can last much longer.

🔧 Our proposal

Keep an inventory, clear device names, configuration backups and a change log. Record responsibilities and diagnostic steps.

🔎 How we would check

Use the documentation to locate a fault. Test a configuration restore in a safe environment.

Think about it: Clear records help the next person solve the problem without starting from zero.

🙂07 / 07

User Experience

Does it help people?

THE PROBLEM

Technical checks look fine. Students still cannot finish their tasks.

Look closer ＋

💬 Why it matters

Users lose time, confidence and access to learning opportunities.

🔧 Our proposal

Try logging in, uploading assignments and joining a class call. Ask students, teachers and staff about their experience.

🔎 How we would check

Record task completion, interruptions and user feedback during typical busy periods.

Think about it: The goal is a usable service, not just a green status light.

OKAY… WHAT SHOULD WE DO FIRST?

Smart priorities. Realistic budget.

We cannot upgrade everything at once. For this case, we would start with the biggest risks and then use evidence to guide spending.

🔐 First: protect access & understand the slowdown

Why first? Information exposure deserves prompt attention, while interrupted classes directly affect learning. We would review access controls and measure the busy-period bottleneck before buying equipment.

🛟 Next: make recovery possible

Why next? A central failure can affect many users. We would document and back up configurations, test recovery and compare the cost of backup power or redundancy with the impact of downtime. A critical availability risk could move this work to first priority.

🌱 Then: prepare for growth & keep listening

Why then? More students need more resources, but unused capacity also costs money. We would plan expansion in stages, maintain documentation and keep checking real tasks and user feedback. These checks continue throughout every phase.

OUR VERDICT

A good network works for people.
Not just for devices.

Internet access is only the starting point. A good solution combines connectivity, performance, security, availability, scalability, management and a positive user experience.

Our reflection: as future systems engineers, we would avoid calling a network “good” after a single successful test. We would ask who uses it, what can fail and what evidence supports our decisions.

Next step: validate the proposals with authorised tests and real user feedback. No improvements or test results are claimed here.
